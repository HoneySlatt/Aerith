# 0002. MPL-2.0

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-11

## Context

The owner's other applications are under GPL-3.0-or-later. Aerith must reach Apple TV and iPhone,
and the App Store is the only way onto Apple TV: Apple's alternative marketplaces cover iOS and
iPadOS in the EU only. The App Store's usage rules add restrictions that the GPL forbids; VLC was
removed from the App Store for this in 2011 and returned in 2013 under MPL-2.0, its engine relicensed
to LGPL ([survey](../survey.md#apple-platforms)). VLC for iOS, Swiftfin and Streamyfin are all
MPL-2.0 on the App Store today.

## Decision

**Aerith is licensed under the Mozilla Public License 2.0** ([`LICENSE`](../../LICENSE)). Each file
carries the MPL-2.0 header.

**What ships inside an Apple or store build is MPL-, LGPL- or permissively licensed**: FFmpeg is
built without `--enable-gpl` and linked dynamically. Code under the GPL never enters Aerith's own
binaries.

**mpv on the desktop runs as a program apart** ([ADR-0003](0003-mpv-in-its-own-process.md)), so its
GPL build ships beside Aerith with its source offered, without binding Aerith's licence.

## Alternatives considered

- **GPL-3.0-or-later with an extra permission for app stores** (the route of AetherEngine): the
  family's licence and strong copyleft, but an exception never tested, and every outside
  contribution would need a transfer of rights.
- **GPL-3.0-or-later alone**: no Apple TV, and no iPhone outside the EU.
- **MIT OR Apache-2.0**, as uwui and Pluto: no friction anywhere, but anyone could make a closed
  product of it.

## Consequences

- Contributions need no transfer of rights; outside contributions are taken when the owner opens
  them.
- The copyleft is per file: a closed program may combine Aerith's unmodified files with its own.
- Nuvio's code (GPL-3.0) cannot be reused; its protocols can be implemented.
- Every dependency's licence is checked by `cargo deny` once the workspace exists.
